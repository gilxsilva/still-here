THE METHOD / THE SOURCE

Open data.
Open book.

We use the SSA 2023 period life table ↗, as used in the 2026 Trustees Report. Retrieved October 18, 2026.

It covers the Social Security area population, which is U.S.-based. See the source for its full definition.

The annual probability q measures dying between exact ages x and x + 1, conditional on reaching x. This prototype supports ages 18–100.

THE METHOD / THE ASSUMPTIONS

A model.
With limits.

Daily risk is modeled as constant within each age-year. Age at last birthday approximates exact age.

We ignore your upcoming birthday, leap years, seasonality, medical history, behavior, and day-specific hazards. This cannot estimate your individual risk.

The age curve compares people who have reached each age under fixed 2023 rates. It is not a forecast of your health or future mortality trends.

THE METHOD / A LITTLE DEEPER

Survival,
multiplied.

The chain rule multiplies daily survival probabilities conditional on being alive at the start of each day. If each is 1 − p, annual survival is (1 − p)365.

λ = −ln(1 − q)   ·   S(t) = e−λt

Here λ is the constant yearly hazard and t is in years. Then p = 1 − e−λ/365. For small q, q ÷ 365 approximates the exact root formula we use.

THE METHOD / THE RANGE

Two tables.
No crystal ball.

The source publishes female and male categories. We show their spread without asking for sex or gender.

That spread is not a confidence interval. It does not bound an individual’s risk, which can differ substantially with health and circumstances.

The table also gives average remaining years conditional on reaching a given age. An average is not a deadline or a personal prediction.

THE METHOD / YOUR PRIVACY

Your inputs.
Your browser.

Your age, weekly budget, and reflection are processed in browser memory. The app sends none of these inputs to a server and saves none between visits.

No app analytics, AI calls, or input-bearing URLs. Reloading clears your exploration.
