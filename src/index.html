HOW IT WORKS / THE DATA

The data.

The numbers come from the SSA 2023 period life table ↗, used in the 2026 Trustees Report. Retrieved October 18, 2026.

It covers the SSA’s U.S.-based Social Security area population. The source lists who is included.

q is the chance of dying between exact ages x and x + 1, given that someone has reached x. This version covers ages 18–100.

HOW IT WORKS / THE ASSUMPTIONS

What we
assume.

We use age at your last birthday to choose a table row. Within that age-year, we assume the daily risk stays constant.

That leaves out health, behavior, seasons, leap years, daily hazards, and when your next birthday falls. Individual risk can be different.

The curve uses 2023 rates at every age and assumes each person has reached that age. Future mortality rates could change.

HOW IT WORKS / THE FORMULA

Why the
formula works.

To survive a year, you have to survive each day. Multiply the daily survival probabilities, each conditional on reaching that day. With a constant rate, that gives (1 − p)365.

λ = −ln(1 − q)   ·   S(t) = e−λt

λ is the constant yearly hazard; t is in years. So p = 1 − e−λ/365. When q is small, q ÷ 365 is close. We use the exact root formula.

HOW IT WORKS / THE RANGE

Why show
a range?

The SSA publishes separate female and male tables. Showing both lets us start with age alone.

The range compares those two groups. It isn’t a confidence interval, and your own risk can fall outside it.

The table also gives average remaining years for people who have reached each age. That average doesn’t tell us how long one person will live.

HOW IT WORKS / YOUR INPUTS

What happens
to your inputs?
